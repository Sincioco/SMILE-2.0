SIN STAR I

Draft Game Script — Version 0.2: Canon Update

Format: Main-story JRPG narrative script with playable sequences, cinematic direction, character notes, and author-review annotations.
Language: English.
Audience: Author / narrative / art / design review. Full-story spoilers.
Status: ✅ End-to-end first-pass story draft. 📋 Dialogue editing, unresolved lore decisions, encounter design, and production implementation remain.
Original draft: September 14, 2026.
Canon update: September 15, 2026. D01–D16 accepted by Sin; D10 clarified below.

The story's promise: A young Hunter who protects himself by loving nobody learns to defend imperfect, temporary lives—including his own—against beings willing to replace them in pursuit of a better existence.

This is a readable first draft of the main narrative, not merely a plot outline. The opening, recruitment, companion conflicts, major revelations, final confrontations, and return home have scripted dialogue. Exploration routes, ordinary enemy encounters, incidental townspeople, and every possible battle bark are not exhaustively scripted. No gameplay duration or engine capability is implied.

The central story reaches an ending. Sin accepted D01–D16 on September 15, 2026, with the D10 clarification that prosecuting Orin's father would expose deeper military corruption. Those decisions are canon. Dialogue, staging and detailed gameplay remain drafts. Remaining open questions are listed individually; prior requests to approve these sixteen choices are superseded.

1. Reading and review guide

What the labels mean

Label Meaning
ESTABLISHED A story decision supplied or explicitly accepted by the author in this conversation.
ACCEPTED CANON — Dxx D01–D16 were explicitly accepted by Sin on September 15, 2026. D10 includes his clarification about suppressed prosecution and deeper military corruption.
OPEN — Qxx A decision the author has deliberately not settled. The draft avoids deciding it, or identifies its temporary staging choice.
SCENE / ACTION What the player sees or hears; not a narrator reading production notes.
PLAY Suggested player activity and narrative encounter objective. These are design proposals, not implemented features.
REVIEW Information for the author, including motivation, continuity, and presentation risks. Not in-game dialogue.
OPTIONAL RESPONSE A small player-expression choice. Unless stated otherwise, responses reconverge without changing established character growth.

Character names in bold precede dialogue. Short italic passages describe acting or camera direction. Dialogue can be divided into additional text boxes during implementation; paragraph breaks indicate natural pauses, not a fixed engine limit. Stage directions must not appear as spoken lines.

Scene IDs are stable review references. An annotation such as “C06-S04: Father needs more hesitation” is sufficient to locate a proposed change.

Character and dramatic guardrails

Arin's power is not what makes his life worth saving. Mira saves him before knowing his ancestry. Orin's courage exists before lightning. Zara is genuinely artificial, not a disguised biological person. Milo is a male, four-legged dog from a spacefaring canine people, not a human in a dog-shaped costume. Kael's compassion explains his conduct without absolving his victims' losses. Aevos is a powerful creator/custodian, not an actual omnipotent god.

No faction is an entire species. Human liberators do not make all humans innocent; artificial enslavers do not make Zara responsible; bereaved villagers are not all violent attackers. The worth of a life does not depend on intelligence, combat usefulness, cosmic ancestry, or its eventual contribution to history.

“Humanity” can describe Arin's capacity for compassion, but dialogue must not imply that compassion is exclusively human. The party itself disproves that proposition.

The story does not prove that suffering is necessary or good. Its heroes continue fighting disease, deprivation, and cruelty. They oppose involuntarily replacing existing people with hypothetical happier successors.

Accepted canon decisions D01–D16

ID Accepted decision First substantial use
D01 The opening occurs in Neris on an unnamed inhabited world. Kael detects the remote resonance while visiting Earth; Arin need not be physically on Earth. C00-S01
D02 Arin's armor is inexpensive Neris surplus, explaining its resemblance to Orin's service armor and Mira's field equipment without inventing military service for Arin. C00-S02
D03 Neris uses a damaged ancient relay to support water and energy infrastructure. Mismanagement, genuine shortages, concealed faults, and Axiom disturbances overlap; none alone explains every crisis. C01-S03
D04 Mira departs with a limited investigative mandate and a relief handover, rather than abandoning her people without arrangements. C01-S04
D05 Veyra is a Kynari frontier/trading world, not the hidden ancestral homeworld. An evacuation introduces Orin and then Zara and Milo. C02-S01
D06 Kynari language is translated by a wearable device. It does not create intelligence. Their spatial/scent-based engineering supplies navigational skills distinct from Zara's systems expertise. C03-S01
D07 Ordinary elemental arts are limited expressions of the Axiom. Voice alone is insufficient: living authority, coherence, and deliberate control matter. Recordings cannot reproduce Arin's effects. C04-S02
D08 Recovered wartime records identify a particular AI combat faction and unit as responsible for the ship attack. Zara is not personally connected to that unit. C05-S01
D09 The family's village and an affected destination are called “the home village” and “East Valley.” Orin is staged as an unwitting beneficiary; exact age, names, and the father's prior knowledge remain open. C06-S01
D10 Orin's father was never prosecuted because doing so would expose deeper corruption within the military. Neris's present supply inquiry exposes obstruction and this institutional suppression. D11 Surviving ancient archives establish imported human ancestral patterns, consciousness participating in reality, and persistent traces after death. This is fictional cosmology, not a claim about real biology or physics. C07-S03
D12 Earlier cosmic renewals belonged to one continuing experiment and preserved Kael. His return is a different termination condition, which would discard him too. C09-S02
D13 Opening access requires two distinct compatible living signatures, not agreement. Rewriting foundations requires sustained cooperation. A protective counter-Axiom accidentally satisfies the access condition. C08-S04
D14 A pretested synchronized field keeps the party aligned with elapsed time at home. It neither freezes the universe nor rewinds the departure instant. C08-S01
D15 At the Origin, severing Aevos's destructive supervisory access preserves the independent sustaining machinery. The party must defeat his intervention without smashing that machinery. C09-S03

Principal open decisions

The old man's identity is intentionally unrevealed. Kael's eventual prognosis, Arin's longevity, literal interpretation of “half human,” the future custodian, and any marriage or child are not settled here. D16 settles this game's staged return: both Arin and critically wounded Kael leave alive; a mandatory life-cost death for Arin is not the accepted ending. The closing review register explains which scenes would change under alternatives.

3. Cast and performance notes

Arin

At entry: A capable but under-equipped Hunter who mistakes isolation for freedom. He has tenderness he works hard not to display. His nightmares and exhaustion increase his impatience; they are not his entire personality.

Voice: Short, practical sentences; dry observations. Early questions concern pay, routes, and survival. Later he asks what others need. Do not make him deliver polished philosophical monologues every time he learns something.

Private want: Never again be helpless while someone he loves dies. Growth: Allow attachment without claiming he can guarantee anyone's safety. Gameplay expression: Learns several elements, but specialists retain skill and judgment he lacks. He cannot solve the last encounter alone.

Mira

At entry: A trained water-arts healer and responsible princess investigating conditions the palace does not adequately see. She is decisive, observant, and occasionally overconfident that asking the right questions will make institutions cooperate.

Voice: Direct and specific. She asks who is missing, where supplies went, and what treatment is available. Warmth comes through attention rather than constant speeches about kindness.

Private want: Become a ruler who deserves accurate answers, not someone people protect from them. Growth: Share responsibility; accept limits without accepting neglect. Relationship note: Never owes Arin romance for protecting him. Never loses political purpose when affection develops.

Orin

At entry: An experienced ex-commander, practical protector, and reluctant recipient of help. Dry humor. He notices fatigue and poor equipment. His unease around home is different from his steadiness under fire.

Voice: Tactical clarity in danger; evasive economy about himself. Distinguish him from Arin through rhythm, posture, and address rather than changing their accepted names.

Private want: Protect people without again lending his courage to a harmful decision. Growth: Face a loved one's wrongdoing without reducing him to it; let others stand beside him. Power note: Lightning extends his abilities. It did not give him courage.

Zara

At entry: Entirely artificial, openly so, a Hunter because she enjoys choosing difficult and interesting work. Her curiosity is personal, not a machine demonstrating it has a soul.

Voice: Precise, playful, sometimes impatient. Humor must not turn her into an infallible lie detector. She can be hurt, angry, mistaken, and slow to trust after an apology.

Private want: Live a life not defined by the uses others intended for her. Growth: Adventure includes remaining for the aftermath. Boundary: She owes nobody an apology for an unrelated AI faction's crimes. Forgiveness is her decision, not Arin's reward for feeling guilty.

Milo

At entry: A male Kynari surveyor/courier with four paws, expressive ears, a keen appetite, and responsibilities to living relatives and community. He is cute without being property.

Voice: Concrete, observant, occasionally very funny. He does not speak only in jokes or vaguely wise proverbs. His translator leaves room for breathing, posture, whines, and barks.

Private want: Explore without giving up the people and places that make returning worthwhile. Growth: Trust selected outsiders without erasing his people's reasons for caution. Staging: Paw/nose-accessible controls, low work surfaces, harness-mounted tools. He does not suddenly grow hands for an important scene.

Kael

At entry: Ancient embodied consciousness, reborn across lives and cycles, carrying the cumulative memory of love and loss. He is not constantly raging. Long stretches of patient attention make his occasional weariness more affecting.

Voice: Almost conversational at first; increasingly urgent when Arin can refuse something he has awaited for ages. He knows much more than the party, but not everything. His predictions are inferences, never reliable future sight.

Private want: End involuntary loss and make existence worthy of continuing. Fatal error: Treat replacements as rescues and lives as negotiable inputs. Change: Arin's emergence challenges his certainty; he finally protects existing people without requiring proof of eternal benefit.

Aevos

At entry: An ancient creator/custodian from a civilization that has not solved its own suffering. Calm, attentive, and capable of sincere concern. He uses the word “care” without recognizing the ownership it assumes.

Voice: Plain and exact, not snarling. He should sound as though he is explaining a painful but responsible procedure. Let the threat come from what he is prepared to do, not from insults.

Dramatic function: Offers Kael's argument back to him at a larger scale. Limit: He controls the Origin's supervisory access, not existence by personal heartbeat. He can be defeated locally without proving the creators' whole civilization has been conquered.

Supporting roles

ORIN'S FATHER: A genuinely respected former officer who diverted supplies, saved his village, and cannot reconcile the gratitude with the losses elsewhere. His love is real; so is his responsibility.

ORIN'S MOTHER: A partner choosing safety while sustaining her marriage privately. Neither absolving witness nor passive victim. Her knowledge before exposure is not invented here.

ORIN'S SISTER: Maintains contact with her brother and respects her parents' privacy. Does not manipulate a reconciliation. Name and profession remain open.

KING / QUEEN OF NERIS: Mira's living parents. Concerned with continuity and order; not automatically innocent because reports were filtered. Exact responsibility remains reviewable.

MARSHAL: A working antagonist in Neris's present administration. A role label pending naming. His conduct does not retroactively account for every past crime.

OLD MAN: Genuinely rescued by Orin in the visible version. Irritable, observant, physically vulnerable. No confirmed identity or comprehensive exposition.

Prologue — Room for One

C00-S01 — The dream that follows him

Location / time: Cheap Hunter lodging in Neris; late night.
Cast: Arin; fragmented impressions of his parents; an unidentified opponent in the dream.
Player objective: Learn movement, guard, and recovery without consuming inventory.
Source / review: S01–S03. D01. The ship memory is impressionistic; it does not establish the exact mechanics of Arin's childhood survival. The earliest opponent is a dream image, not confirmed future sight or evidence that Kael has already contacted him.

SCENE

Black screen. A loose fitting rattles against metal. A woman's hand fastens a strap around a child's wrist. We do not see her whole face.

MOTHER: Too tight?

YOUNG ARIN: No.

FATHER: He'd say that if you tied him to the chair.

A brief laugh. Arin remembers the laugh clearly.

The sound becomes a warning chime. Light flashes across a viewport. A shape beyond the glass disappears inside white fire.

No graphic bodies. No final parental speech explaining the plot.

Arin runs toward a door that moves farther away with each step.

ARIN: Wait—

The door opens onto a vast, dark floor. An indistinct figure stands beyond its reflected light.

PLAY

A short dream encounter teaches defensive input. The enemy's attack patterns are readable; the player can improve, but the scene ends after a fixed small number of exchanges. No consumables, lost currency, or false promise that grinding can defeat this opponent. Arin's attacks do not reach far enough.

ARIN: Who are you?

The figure does not answer. A line of light divides the floor.

Arin raises his weapon. His mouth forms a sound that is not subtitled as an ordinary word.

Cut to the lodging room. A cup moves across the table without being touched. A hairline crack travels up the plaster.

Arin wakes with one hand extended.

ARIN: ...No.

Someone pounds on the wall.

NEIGHBOR: Some of us work in the morning!

ARIN: So do I.

He lowers his hand before noticing what it is doing. The cup stops against a ledger listing rent and armor repairs.

He opens a small compartment. Inside is the worn wrist strap from the dream. He touches it, then closes the compartment before the music can become comforting.

A second pillow lies unused on the bed. He takes it off and wedges it against the rattling window.

TRANSITION: Dawn. The crack remains in the wall.

C00-S02 — A contract, not a calling

Location / time: Hunters' Guild counter and repair stall; morning.
Cast: Arin; guild clerk; armorer.
Player objective: Accept the waterworks contract and learn the cost of equipment maintenance.
Source / review: S01, D02. Role names are temporary. Arin is not a former Neris soldier.

SCENE

The armorer holds Arin's shoulder plate against the light.

ARMORER: This is Neris issue.

ARIN: It was Neris issue. Then it was a box behind a counter. Then it was mine.

ARMORER: The rivets remember the first owner.

ARIN: Can they pay for the repair?

The armorer points to the cracked fastening.

ARMORER: I can replace that much. The rest needs proper work.

Arin counts coins twice. The result does not change.

At the guild counter, the clerk turns a contract toward him.

CLERK: Night inspection. Lower waterworks. Missing survey crew. Recover their route recorder if the route is unsafe.

ARIN: Recovery fee?

CLERK: If the recorder is intact.

ARIN: And the crew?

CLERK: A rescue allowance. If they're alive.

He studies the line for equipment damage.

CLERK: There are group contracts.

ARIN: Then split one between a group.

CLERK: You could join a group, Arin.

ARIN: I heard you.

He signs.

OPTIONAL RESPONSE — clerk asks for an emergency contact

  • “Leave it blank.”
  • “The guild will have my account.”

Either response leads to the clerk setting the field aside without a joke.

PLAY

A short town route establishes closed bakeries, a public water queue, soldiers guarding an orderly distribution point, and people who disagree about why shortages are occurring. One soldier gives a child part of her own ration. Another turns a sick laborer away because his permit is for a different district.

Do not explain the whole kingdom through one villainous guard.

Arin passes the water queue. He checks the time on his contract and continues.

C00-S03 — A familiar sky, an unfamiliar sound

Location / time: Earth; a quiet hillside at night.
Cast: Kael, not yet named to the player.
Player objective: None; brief cinematic.
Source / review: S03, S11; D01. Earth is a place he protects. His ultimate origin is not revealed.

SCENE

A man watches a town below the hillside. Music comes faintly from an open window. Someone laughs badly at someone else's joke.

Far above, a bright object changes course by an almost imperceptible degree. Kael lowers two fingers. The people below never look up.

He turns away.

Then he stops.

A sound from Arin's dream reaches him—not as ordinary airborne speech, but as a disturbance briefly visible in the stars' reflections in his eyes.

He repeats part of it under his breath. Nothing happens. He is comparing, not casting.

KAEL: That wasn't mine.

He looks toward a region of sky with no special star marked for the player.

Cut before he explains or acts.

TITLE CARD: SIN STAR I

Chapter 01 — The Water Under Neris

C01-S01 — Someone comes back

Location / time: Lower Neris waterworks; night.
Cast: Arin; Mira in plain traveling clothes; a trapped surveyor.
Player objective: Find the survey team and escape an activated maintenance defense.
Source / review: S04. Mira's intervention is established; specific encounter is D03. The defense is non-sentient equipment, not an excuse for indiscriminate violence against conscious artificial people.

SCENE / PLAY

Arin finds abandoned measuring equipment beside a channel that should contain clean water. The water is cloudy and warm.

A surveyor is pinned behind a damaged maintenance frame. Arin tries to move it; a defense assembly wakes when he lifts the recorder.

The fight begins as a manageable contract encounter. Then the floor gives way, leaving Arin below the outlet of a pressurized conduit. His damaged shoulder fastening snaps.

He tries to stand. His leg will not carry him.

ARIN: Come on. Come on—

The conduit opens.

A curved wall of water rises between him and the descending metal. It holds for a heartbeat, then hardens into a turning sheet that throws the assembly aside.

Mira drops from the access stairs.

MIRA: Can you move your foot?

ARIN: I was getting to that.

MIRA: Try now.

She presses two fingers near the wound. Water gathers around the injury, drawing heat away and stabilizing it. He gasps.

ARIN: You could have warned me.

MIRA: You could have answered.

She helps him up without waiting for a statement of gratitude.

ARIN: That thing isn't finished.

MIRA: Neither are we. Take its attention off the upper joint.

PLAY

Mira temporarily joins. A cooperative tutorial makes her healing and redirection essential. Arin strikes the exposed mechanism; she contains the pressure release. The surveyor can then be reached.

The defeated assembly loses power. No victory fanfare yet: someone is still hurt.

ARIN: Why were you down here?

MIRA: Looking for the water everyone keeps telling me is safe.

She lifts the surveyor's arm around her shoulder.

ARIN: The upper stairs are clear. We can get out.

MIRA: He can't use stairs like this. Find a board we can carry.

Arin looks at the exit, then at the surveyor. He puts away the recorder and starts searching.

C01-S02 — No one asked her to stay

Location / time: An improvised street clinic near the waterworks; before dawn.
Cast: Arin; Mira; clinic attendant; patients.
Player objective: Deliver the injured surveyor and assist with a practical task.
Source / review: S04. This is the first small behavioral change, not an instant conversion to selfless heroism.

SCENE

Mira is washing the surveyor's wound. Her borrowed sleeves are rolled beyond expensive-looking stitching she has forgotten to hide.

ATTENDANT: We can manage from here.

MIRA: Where is your clean storage?

The attendant does not answer immediately.

Mira looks at the half-empty containers.

MIRA: Show me what you have.

Arin checks his recorder. It survived. He can collect the fee.

ARIN: You're not getting paid for this, are you?

MIRA: No.

ARIN: They have people for this.

MIRA: Two. One has been awake since yesterday.

She does not look up.

Appendix B — Ending variants and sequel boundaries

B01 — Primary draft: critical survival, no promised recovery

This is the staged main ending in E01-S01. Kael leaves the Origin alive but mortally wounded and under exceptional support. “Alive at the last observation” is not a confirmed long-term survival outcome. He does not demonstrate renewed galaxy-altering ability, reveal that the wound was a test, or resume giving flawless instructions remotely.

Narrative advantage: The author retains the choice between later death and lasting incapacity without claiming Arin has inherited a resurrection mechanism. The current victory and return are complete even while the prognosis is not.

Required sequel discipline: Decide his surviving capacities before writing Sin Star II's main conflict. A mentor with bounded knowledge is different from an omnipotent emergency contact. Loss of power must not be treated as loss of personhood; he can remain morally accountable and personally significant without serving as the next game's strongest fighter.

B02 — Superseded comparison: Kael dies before the return

This comparison is not the accepted D16 ending. Do not apply it without a new direction from Sin. It would replace the last portion of E01-S01, after the verified records are secured. All previous party contributions and Aevos's defeat remain unchanged.

No light enters Arin. No unseen system announces a successor. No new heartbeat immediately promises another incarnation.

The party carries his body home within the prepared field. Arin's grief does not prevent the others from helping him do what must be done next.

Dependent edits: E01-S01 loses its “alive” prognosis; E01-S02 gains a restrained remembrance chosen by the author. Do not announce universal forgiveness, erase his victims, or make his grave an automatic royal monument. The sequel must not reverse this death casually. Whether any persistence beyond bodily death remains possible is a separate author decision, not a promised twist.

B03 — Arin's life-cost Axiom is not selected in this draft

The earlier possibility that Arin pays with his life is a superseded comparison for this game. D16 accepts his survival; changing that requires a new direction from Sin. In the primary script, he risks death but is not required to die. The distinction allows him to demonstrate courage while also applying the lessons of restraint and cooperation.

Selecting a life-cost ending would require rewriting C09-S05 and all three epilogue scenes. It would also require answering whose informed choice triggers the cost, why the others cannot help distribute or avoid it, and whether the player is falsely promised resurrection. Do not simply add a death after the existing victory as an emotional surcharge.

No ending here automatically transfers “The One,” immortality, or an obligation to become custodian to Arin.

B04 — What the first game deliberately does not spend

Sin Star II may develop Arin's greater command of the Axiom, Mira's path to the throne, their relationship, marriage, or a child. Those possibilities are not closing revelations in Sin Star I. A child's value must not be reduced to the power predicted from two parents. Similarly, a future custodian is a possibility outside the current victory, not evidence that the party accomplished nothing.

Chosen gateway explanation

The access assembly normally pairs an outside invitation with the witness's living signature. Arin supplies a compatible independent signature from inside, an unplanned route the original arrangement did not anticipate. Kael knew that Arin made a two-speaker intervention possible; he did not know the diagnostic purpose of his homeward compulsion. Aevos had not recalled him in person during earlier renewals.

Opening access is weaker than rewriting existence. Arin's “Hold” can complete an access circuit while remaining a genuine refusal of the Second Dawn. It does not mean that protecting his friends secretly counts as consent to their destruction. Aevos independently chooses to treat the unexpected return as sufficient evidence to terminate this version.

Battle and presentation rules

Dream duels are short, resource-free, and do not conceal an attainable victory. Veyra's encounter advertises evacuation. The Kael battle is genuinely winnable by the party through clear focus objectives. Aevos's first movement is a clearly staged interruption/survival challenge, followed by his escalation—not twenty minutes of meaningless damage against an unwinnable health bar.

Allow a preparation checkpoint before the final sequence and a non-disruptive retry checkpoint at the Origin battle. No ending depends on hoarding a consumable whose need was never communicated. The exact combat system, turn structure, skill names, numerical balance, and UI remain design work.

Aevos's four arms, worlds, and hourglasses should create readable encounter changes rather than become unrelated boss phases. Never confuse the destructive control link with the life-sustaining structure. Do not use the disappearance of displayed worlds as proof that inhabited planets were sacrificed to make the ending spectacular.

Companion skills supplement one another. Milo does not become a computer terminal with paws; Zara cannot hack unfamiliar reality instantly; Mira cannot heal every category of injury; Orin's lightning does not remove the need for tactics; Arin cannot turn every request into an unrestricted command.
