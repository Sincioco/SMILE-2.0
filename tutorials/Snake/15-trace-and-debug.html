Trace One Frame and Debug Methodically

Professional programmers follow the data in order and inspect the small stage that owns the problem.

InputStateDirectionTimeUpdateRulesDraw

Trace one complete frame

MomentQuestionWhat Snake does
1. InputDid the player press a key?Get Key Key
2. StateWhich screen are we in?Choose the State 0, 1, or 2 branch.
3. DirectionIs the requested turn safe?Update NextDirection only when it is not opposite.
4. TimeIs it time for another move?Compare Timer() with NextMoveTime.
5. UpdateWhere does each segment go?Call MoveSnake().
6. RulesWall, body, or food?End the round or grow and score.
7. DrawWhat does the current data look like?Draw title, board, and perhaps the result panel.
8. PresentCan the player see it?Show Screen.

Example trace

Before: head (17,12), moving right, food (18,12), score 0. The body shifts; head X becomes 18. Head and food match, so Length becomes 6, Score becomes 10, Eat.wav plays, new food spawns, and the next move is scheduled.

Test checklist

  • Title screen appears
  • Enter begins a round
  • WASD and arrows turn
  • Opposite direction is rejected
  • Food never appears in the body
  • Eating grows by one and adds 10
  • Walls and body collision end the round
  • Game-over panel appears
  • Enter retries
  • High score saves and reloads
  • Sounds play from Assets
  • Escape exits; Alt+Enter toggles full screen

Bug-to-owner map

SymptomFirst place to inspect
Snake turns backward into itselfMain-loop direction conditions
Body becomes copies of the headThe Down To body-shift loop
Food appears under the bodySpawnFood overlap test
Snake draws in the wrong placeGrid-to-pixel formulas in DrawBoard
Score changes but display does notDraw Number Score and Show Screen
Game over occurs too earlyWall and self-collision conditions
Sound is silentAsset path, copied Assets folder, and application focus

Controlled bug experiment

After saving a good copy, temporarily change the body-shift loop from Down To to To. Predict the result, observe it, then restore the correct line. Controlled experiments turn bugs into lessons.
