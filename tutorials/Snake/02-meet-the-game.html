Meet the Game

Snake is a native Windows game built from rectangles, text, numbers, keyboard input, timing, sound, and saved storage.

35 × 24 gridWASD or arrows3 game states

Player job

Use WASD or the arrow keys. Guide the head toward red food. Avoid the walls and the snake's own body.

Computer job

Remember every segment, move them in order, place food safely, detect collisions, draw the next picture, and save the high score.

Your job

Understand the model: a grid, two coordinate arrays, a direction, a timer, a score, and a game state.

Snake teaches the core game pattern

Input → Update → Check → Draw → Show → Repeat. The games later in the SMILE series become larger, but this same thinking pattern stays with you.

Break the big question apart

Instead of asking “How do I create a whole game?”, ask: Where is the head? Where is the food? What key was pressed? Is the next square legal? What should be drawn now?

Repetition builds memory

Reinforce what you learned

Say it again

The play field is a 35 × 24 grid. Each grid square is 20 × 20 pixels.

Recall it

Without looking back, how many grid squares are on the board?

Check your answer

840. The same number returns when we declare the two body arrays in Checkpoint 1.
